# Week 1, Session A: Defend the Jab and Answer Immediately

Duration: 60 minutes

Objective: see the jab, distinguish attack from feint, use a compact rear-hand parry or catch, counter immediately, and recover position.

Equipment: gloves, mitts, mouthguards, timer, and four boundary markers. Contact ceiling: technical pace at approximately 20–30% power.

## 0:00–8:00: shadowboxing diagnostic

- 2 minutes stance and directional movement.
- 2 minutes jab and 1–2 while moving.
- 2 minutes loaded slips into cross or hook, checking prior glute/core corrections.
- 2 minutes free shadowboxing with today's exact counters:
  - rear-hand parry → immediate cross;
  - rear-hand catch → jab-cross;
  - recover stance or take a small angle exit.

Do not add hooks to today's defensive-hand shadow pattern. Repair prior skills only if they visibly fail.

## 8:00–18:00: partner glove drilling

Both wear gloves. Sameer feeds; no mitts.

- 2 minutes predictable single jabs, parry only.
- 2 minutes predictable single jabs, catch only.
- 2 minutes Sameer calls parry or catch before each jab.
- 2 minutes no call; Aneek chooses.
- 2 minutes every defense produces an immediate jab or cross at touch power.

Parry cue: make the jab miss by an inch, not leave the room.

Catch cue: let the jab come to the shield; do not chase it.

Failed repetition: eyes close, hand reaches or swats, stance breaks, lead hand drops, or the counter pauses.

## 18:00–32:00: mitt/pad rounds

Sameer presents one mitt as the incoming jab, then presents the counter target only after the defense.

Four two-minute rounds with approximately 30 seconds between rounds:

1. parry → jab;
2. parry → cross;
3. catch → jab-cross;
4. randomized parry/catch → jab, cross, or 1–2 → angle exit.

The pad block builds clean sequencing and accuracy. It does not prove live recognition.

## 32:00–42:00: reaction drilling

Both wear gloves and mouthguards.

- 3 minutes variable single jabs; defense plus one straight counter.
- 3 minutes single or double jab; defend the complete attack before returning up to two punches.
- 4 minutes jab, double jab, feint, and boundary pressure; defend only real attacks, counter, and exit laterally.

This remains a controlled reaction drill because Sameer owns the attack menu.

## 42:00–56:00: constraint-led technical sparring

Allow about 3 minutes for rules and gear, then run four two-minute rounds with one minute between rounds.

### Round 1: single jab only

Sameer varies cadence, range, and entry. Aneek parries/catches, returns one jab or cross, and resets.

### Round 2: single or double jab

Aneek may return up to a 1–2 after defending the complete attack. Test whether he counters too early into the second jab.

### Round 3: jab versus feint

Sameer may jab, feint, or step into range. Aneek scores by defending real punches without parrying empty air, then countering.

### Round 4: pressure, counter, and exit

Sameer pressures with single/double jabs and feints. Aneek defends, counters, and exits left or right. More than one straight backward step is a failed exchange.

Sameer may spam the target problem but must vary timing, distance, and cadence so Aneek learns perception rather than a memorized rhythm.

## 56:00–60:00: gate and writeback

Aneek must complete five consecutive clean exchanges with:

- correct jab/feint recognition;
- compact parry or catch;
- immediate counter;
- eyes open and controlled breathing;
- stance preserved;
- controlled contact;
- angle exit under pressure.

Record the fields listed in `ledger/aneek-training-state.json`. If mechanics work on pads but fail in reaction drilling, Session B keeps its new material but begins with a short reaction repair block. If mechanics fail during predictable drilling, repair the specific mechanic before adding complexity.
